Understanding Kidney Disease

Kidney disease affects how well your kidneys filter waste and excess fluids from your blood. When renal filters (nephrons) become damaged, toxins accumulate in the bloodstream, leading to serious systemic complications.

Major Causes & Progression

Kidney disease is often initiated by underlying chronic conditions like diabetes and high blood pressure. Left unmonitored, the damage typically progresses over months or years. Symptoms often include swelling in the extremities, nausea, persistent fatigue, and restless sleep.

Common Classifications:

  • Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD): Gradual, irreversible loss of renal function categorized across 5 stages based on eGFR.
  • Acute Kidney Injury (AKI): Sudden reduction in kidney function, often reversible with prompt hospital care.
  • Polycystic Kidney Disease (PKD): Genetic disorder causing cysts to form and enlarge inside kidney parenchyma.
  • Lupus Nephritis: Autoimmune attack on kidney tissue causing inflammation and protein loss.
