What Are Kidneys? General Knowledge & Anatomy

Kidneys are two bean-shaped organs located in the back, one on each side of the spine, that filter blood and produce urine to remove metabolic waste. They also help regulate blood pressure, stimulate red blood cell production, and maintain optimal fluid and electrolyte balance throughout the human body.

Location

Situated in the retroperitoneal space in the back, just below the rib cage, on either side of the spine. The right kidney typically sits slightly lower than the left to accommodate the liver.

Core Functions

  • Blood Filtration: Remove waste products, excess water, acids, and foreign substances from the blood, producing urine.
  • Fluid & Electrolyte Balance: Maintain the precise balance of water, sodium, potassium, calcium, and phosphate in the bloodstream.
  • Hormone Production: Produce erythropoietin (EPO) which stimulates red blood cell creation, and renin which controls systemic blood pressure.
  • Vitamin D Activation: Synthesize active calcitriol to maintain strong bones and healthy calcium absorption.

Kidney Internal Structure

  • Renal Cortex: The outer layer of the kidney containing blood-filtering glomeruli.
  • Renal Medulla: The inner layer of the kidney, containing renal pyramids that collect and channel urine.
  • Nephrons: Over one million microscopic functional filtering units within each kidney.
  • Renal Pelvis & Ureters: The funnel-like basin and muscular ducts that carry urine down into the bladder for storage and excretion.
