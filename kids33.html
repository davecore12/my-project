mayths and facts

Many common beliefs about kidney disease are inaccurate. For instance, kidney disease is not always associated with back or side pain, and it doesn't always show symptoms until late stages. Furthermore, early diagnosis and treatment can often slow or halt the progression of the disease, even if a cure isn't always possible. Here's a breakdown of common myths and the facts about kidney disease: Myths and Facts about Kidney Disease:

Myth:

Kidney disease is rare.

Fact:

Kidney disease is quite common, affecting millions globally, with about 10% of the world's population suffering from Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD).

Myth:

Kidney disease is always accompanied by back or side pain.

Fact:

Fact: While kidney infections, stones, or cysts can cause pain, kidney disease often progresses silently, with no noticeable symptoms in the early stages.

Myth:

Kidney disease is curable.

Fact:

Fact: Chronic kidney disease (CKD) cannot be cured, but with early diagnosis and treatment, the progression of the disease and the development of renal failure can be slowed or halted.

Myth:

Dialysis is a death sentence.

Fact:

Fact: Dialysis is a life-sustaining treatment for end-stage kidney disease, but it's not a death sentence. With proper management of physical health and adherence to the dialysis schedule, individuals can live long and fulfilling lives.

Myth:

Kidney failure can occur if one kidney fails.

Fact:

Fact: Kidney failure typically occurs when both kidneys are significantly impaired.

Myth:

You'd know if you had kidney disease.

Fact:

Fact: Many people with kidney disease, especially in the early stages, experience no symptoms. Regular check-ups and kidney function tests are crucial for early detection.

Myth:

All kidney diseases are incurable.

Fact:

Fact: While some kidney diseases are irreversible and progressive, with early diagnosis and treatment, many can be cured or their progression slowed down.

Myth:

High urine output indicates healthy kidneys.

Fact:

Fact: In the early stages of kidney disorders, the kidneys' functionality can decrease, leading to increased urine output, and the person may also experience frequent urination at night.

Myth:

If you feel fine, you don't have a kidney problem.

Fact:

Fact: Many people with CKD experience no symptoms in the early stages, and some may not notice any changes in their overall health until the disease has progressed significantly.

Myth:

Dialysis will become a permanent need once it's started.

Fact:

Fact: While dialysis is necessary for some patients with end-stage kidney disease, it may not be a permanent need for everyone. With proper treatment, acute kidney failure can often be reversed, and dialysis may only be required for a short period.

Myth:

You can stop treatment/medication once you start feeling better.

Fact:

Fact: Stopping treatment or medications can be dangerous and may cause a worsening of kidney function, potentially leading to an earlier need for dialysis or transplantation.

Myth:

Detoxification can prevent kidney disease.

Fact:

Fact: The kidneys are naturally responsible for filtering and detoxifying the body, and there is no scientific evidence to support the need for additional detoxification or cleansing methods.

Myth:

Sodium phosphate can detox the kidneys.

Fact:

Fact: Sodium phosphate is used for cleaning the colon, and taking it as a drug can be dangerous for people with kidney disease.

Myth:

Kidney disease is genetic and cannot be prevented.

Fact:

Fact: While genetic factors can increase the risk of kidney disease, many underlying conditions like high blood pressure, obesity, and diabetes, as well as lifestyle factors like diet, exercise, and smoking, are also linked to kidney disease.
