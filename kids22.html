all about kidney health statics

Kidney disease is a significant global health concern, affecting a large portion of the adult population and
leading to premature deaths. It's estimated that 1 in 10 adults have some form of kidney damage, and millions
die annually from related complications. This is particularly true for older adults, where the prevalence of
Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is significantly higher.
Here's a more detailed look at the statistics:

Prevalence and Incidence:

Global:

1 in 10 adults have kidney damage.

Older Adults:

Approximately 1 in 5 men and 1 in 4 women between 65 and 74, and half of those 75 and older, have CKD.

U.S. Specific:

Kidney disease is the fastest-growing noncommunicable disease in the U.S., and one of the top 10 causes of death, even when accounting for COVID-19.

U.S. Diabetes and High Blood Pressure:

About 1 in 3 adults with diabetes and 1 in 5 adults with high blood pressure may have kidney disease.

U.S. Racial/Ethnic Disparities:

Compared to white Americans, Black Americans are 4 times more likely, Hispanic Americans 2 times more likely, Native Americans 2 times more likely, and Asian Americans 1.4 times more likely to develop kidney failure.

U.S. New Kidney Failure Cases:

In 2022, roughly 131,000 Americans were newly diagnosed with kidney failure, with the vast majority (97%) starting dialysis.

Risk Factors:
  • Diabetes: The leading cause of kidney failure, accounting for nearly half of new cases.
  • High Blood Pressure: The second most common cause of kidney failure.
  • Age: CKD is more common in people aged 65 and older.
  • Racial/Ethnic Groups: Black, Hispanic, Native American, and Asian Americans are at higher risk.
    • Other Important Facts:
  • Silent Killer: Kidney disease often has no symptoms until the late stages.
  • Dialysis and Transplants: Many individuals with kidney failure rely on dialysis or kidney transplants.
  • Importance of Early Detection: Early detection and management of CKD can help prevent or slow down progression to end-stage kidney disease.
