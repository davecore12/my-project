All About Kidney Health Statistics

Kidney disease is a significant global health concern, affecting a large portion of the adult population and leading to premature deaths. An estimated 1 in 10 adults worldwide have some form of kidney damage, and millions die annually from related complications without early detection.

Prevalence & Incidence Data

  • Global Scope: 1 in 10 adults globally suffer from chronic kidney damage.
  • Older Adults (65+): Approximately 1 in 5 men and 1 in 4 women aged 65 to 74, and half of adults 75 and older, live with CKD.
  • United States Impact: Kidney disease is one of the top 10 leading causes of death and the fastest-growing noncommunicable disease in the nation.
  • Diabetes & High Blood Pressure: About 1 in 3 adults with diabetes and 1 in 5 adults with high blood pressure suffer from kidney complications.

Racial & Ethnic Disparities

Due to genetic susceptibilities and healthcare access inequities, minority populations experience heightened risk of kidney failure:

  • Black Americans: 4 times more likely to develop kidney failure than white Americans.
  • Hispanic Americans: 2 times more likely to develop kidney failure.
  • Native Americans: 2 times more likely to develop kidney failure.
  • Asian Americans: 1.4 times more likely to develop kidney failure.

Critical Takeaways

  • The "Silent Killer": Kidney disease often presents with zero symptoms until 80-90% of renal capacity is gone.
  • Importance of Early Detection: Routine urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) and blood creatinine (eGFR) tests save lives.
